# Accessibility

Target: **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**.

Dark mode audited 2026-09-26; the rest 2026-09-25. Both against a production build, by scripted inspection of the
live DOM rather than by reading the source — computed contrast against
resolved backgrounds, measured target rectangles, and live-region mutation
counts taken while the page was actually ticking.

This records what was checked, what was found, and — at the end — what was
**not** checked, so a green list here is not mistaken for a full audit.

## What was wrong, and is now fixed

### A live region that announced once per second

The worst of the three, by a distance.

`.market-pulse` carried `role="status" aria-live="polite"` and contained a
countdown — `Prices reshape in {n}s` — driven by a clock that re-renders every
second. A screen reader therefore re-announced the entire strip, including the
market tone, the demand multiplier and the weather, **once per second, for as
long as the page stayed open.** Nothing else on the page could be read over
it.

The live semantics are gone. The information is ambient, it is repeated on the
cards, and the meaningful summary — _"3 options. Best is Curb Curb Taxi at
$72.40 to $73.90."_ — is announced by a dedicated `sr-only` region that changes
only when the comparison does.

Measured after the fix, with the countdown visibly running: **zero live-region
mutations in six seconds.**

The general rule this leaves behind: **a region that updates on a timer must
not be a live region.** If it ticks, it announces, and a screen reader user
cannot turn it off.

### One heading on the entire page

`<h1>RideLens</h1>`, and nothing else. Both `<section>` elements were unnamed,
so heading navigation and region navigation — the two primary ways a screen
reader user moves around a page without reading it linearly — reached nothing.

Now `h1 RideLens` → `h2 Your route` → `h2 Ride options`, in document order,
with each region labelled by its heading. The two new headings are `sr-only`;
the visual design already communicates the structure sighted.

### A 16×16 target

The auto-refresh checkbox was authored at `width: 16px; height: 16px`, below
the 24×24 that **SC 2.5.8 Target Size (Minimum)** requires — new at AA in WCAG
2.2. The user-agent-default exception does not apply, because the size was set
by us rather than inherited.

The label is the real target, so it now carries `min-height: 24px`. The box
still draws at 16 and the control looks unchanged.

### Focus that could hide under the sticky header

**SC 2.4.11 Focus Not Obscured (Minimum)**, also new at AA in 2.2. The topbar
is `position: sticky`, and scrolling a focused control into view tucked it
underneath — the element had focus and its ring was drawn, and a keyboard user
could see neither.

`.results` already had a `scroll-margin-top` for the jump after a comparison.
`scroll-padding-top` on `html` covers every other scroll-into-view, focus
included.

## What was already right

Worth recording, because it is the part most products get wrong and it was not
an accident here.

- **Focus is visible everywhere.** A global `:focus-visible { outline: 2px
solid; outline-offset: 3px }`, offset outside the control so it lands on the
  page background where it has contrast.
- **Forced colours are handled.** A `@media (forced-colors: active)` block
  strips gradients and glass effects and restores system colours, so Windows
  High Contrast does not erase the interface.
- **Reduced motion is respected**, in three places.
- **Every interactive control has an accessible name.** Zero unlabelled
  elements across the comparison page — checked by resolving `aria-label`,
  `title`, text content, `<label>` association and `aria-labelledby` in turn.
- **Text contrast passes.** Two apparent failures were both false positives
  from a script that could not resolve a gradient background: the primary
  button is white on `#2c5a42`, and the other is MapLibre's own scroll
  overlay.
- **Landmarks are complete** — `header`, `nav`, `main`, `footer` — with
  `lang="en"` and a skip link.
- **The map is supplementary.** Its canvas is a labelled region with named
  zoom controls, and no task requires it. **SC 2.5.7 Dragging Movements** is
  therefore satisfied by the map not being load-bearing rather than by the map
  offering an alternative to panning — worth knowing before anything is moved
  into it.

## Both schemes

Dark mode landed 2026-09-26 (see `docs/PERFORMANCE.md` for why the map
rebuilds rather than restyles). It was contrast-audited the same way as
light: every text node on the comparison page and `/sources`, against its
resolved background, at the AA thresholds.

**Dark: zero failures on both pages.** Two were found and fixed on the way
there, and both are the same mistake in different clothes — a colour token
that describes a _shade_ rather than a _role_:

- `.insight-text` used `--forest-3`, a near-black, on a mint surface. Correct
  in light, 1.44:1 in dark. `--mint-t` means "text on mint" and follows the
  surface into dark, which is the whole point of having it.
- The primary button's accent inverts between schemes — dark green in light,
  light mint in dark — and its white label did not, scoring 2.04:1. `--on-accent`
  inverts with it.

The rule this leaves behind: **pick the token for the job, not the colour.**
`--ink` on `--paper`, `--mint-t` on `--mint-bg`, `--on-accent` on the accent.
A token chosen because it happened to look right is a token that will be
wrong in the other scheme.

`npm run contrast` now resolves gradient backgrounds instead of walking past
them, which removed four standing false positives — the primary button's
white label on its gradient, and MapLibre's own overlay among them. **All six
OS × choice combinations report zero failures.**

Six, not two, because the scheme switch has three states rather than two and
the explicit choice beats the system preference — an override the OS can
overrule is not an override. It carries its state in its `aria-label` rather
than only in a glyph.

### Written twice means it can drift

Every dark value is written twice, once behind `prefers-color-scheme: dark`
and once behind `:root[data-theme="dark"]`. That is deliberate, and it is
also a standing hazard: when only one of the two gets a new token, four of
the six combinations still look right and the two that do not are the ones
nobody screenshots.

`--scrim-rgb` reached only the media block. A dark OS was fine. Choosing
**Dark** on a light OS fell through to the light value, and the surfaces that
read it — the sticky topbar among them — stayed light under light ink.

Three more of the same shape were found with it:

- `.prov-sheet` read `var(--porcelain, #f8f6f1)`. `--porcelain` is defined
  nowhere, so the light fallback was the only value it ever had.
- `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce)` forced `.gl` panels to
  white with `!important`, bypassing the tokens the rest of the file reads. A
  dark-mode reader who asks for opaque panels got white ones under light ink.
- `::selection` was mint at half alpha: 13.98:1 over the light ground, 4.18:1
  over the dark one.

`npm run tokens` (in `verify`) now refuses all three shapes statically: a
property in one dark block and not the other, a `var()` whose name has no
producer in CSS or JS, and a property defined in terms of itself — which is
what `--btn-1: var(--btn-1)` was, a find-and-replace that took the background
off every primary button in light mode.

## Non-text contrast, SC 1.4.11

`npm run contrast` walks text nodes. Every one of the guards passed on a
Breakdown bar whose ride slice measured **1.01:1** against its fees slice —
the panel whose whole job is showing where the money goes was a single-tone
bar, and nothing in the repository could tell, because nothing was comparing
a colour against the colour beside it.

`npm run fills` does. It opens all eight deck panels in all six OS × choice
combinations and measures **762 pairs** of two kinds:

- **adjacent** — two sibling fills whose boxes touch, with no border,
  outline, inset ring or gap between them: the segments of a stacked bar.
- **in track** — a fill inside a larger fill it does not cover: a band in its
  ruler, a step in its rail. A bar whose end you cannot find is a bar with no
  length, which is the whole of what it says.

The floor is SC 1.4.11's 3:1, and the exemption list is empty. What the first
run found, all of it in the light scheme and all of it the same mistake:

- `--mint-3` is the bright mint the accent glows are mixed from, and it was
  also being used as a **fill** — the leader's band in Spread, the freshness
  dot, the pinned dot, the eyebrow's dot, the confidence steps. Against the
  tracks those sit in it is **1.7:1**. `--amber-fill` was **2.4:1**. Both
  read in dark, which is the scheme they were chosen in.
- `.axis-bar[data-overlaps="false"]` — the bands that do not overlap the
  leader — was `--ink-3` at `opacity: 0.55`: **2.23:1** in light and **2.74**
  in dark. The comment above it documents an earlier version of the same
  defect, fixed one step and left one step short.

The rule this leaves behind, next to _pick the token for the job_: **a fill
and a text colour are different jobs even when they are the same hue.** An
accent has to climb in dark to carry 4.5:1 as text, and the value that does
that is too light to be a fill in light. `--mint-fill`, `--amber-fill` and
`--sky-fill` exist because of it.

The first version of the exemption list is worth recording too: it exempted
anything whose class ended in `-track`, meaning to let the unfilled remainder
of a bar read as unfilled — and it matched `axis-bar | axis-track`, which is
a band against its own ruler at 2.13:1, the exact thing the file exists to
find. **An exemption written as a pattern exempts what it matches, not what
it meant.**

## Touch targets

Audited 2026-09-26 on an emulated iPhone 13, iPad and desktop, measuring every
`a`, `button`, `input`, `select`, `textarea`, `[role=button]` and `summary`
against the right floor for its pointer.

**44px on a coarse pointer, 24px on a fine one.** The split is deliberate:
44px is a measurement of a fingertip, so it is gated on `(pointer: coarse)`
rather than on viewport width — a narrow window on a laptop does not need
thumb-sized chips, and a tablet in landscape does. 24px is what WCAG 2.2
SC 2.5.8 asks for at AA, and everything clears it with a mouse.

Raising `min-height` rather than expanding hit areas with a pseudo-element,
because the chips wrap: an invisible box grown past its layout would overlap
the row beneath it, and whichever came later in the DOM would quietly steal
the tap.

Result: **0 below floor on all three**, except MapLibre's attribution toggle,
which is left at 24 on purpose — see below.

Found on the way, and worth recording because none of it was guessable:

- The `summary` disclosures that open each breakdown were 20px, which failed
  even the 24px desktop floor. They were the only control in the app failing
  on a mouse.
- Several controls carried higher-specificity rules — `.quick-target .chip`,
  `.route-actions .ghost` — that silently outranked a blanket rule. The
  browser's own `CSS.getMatchedStylesForNode` settled which rule was winning
  faster than reading the file did.
- The theme toggle hides its label under 720px, leaving a 34px-wide button.
- MapLibre ships 29px zoom controls. They are in our DOM and on our map, so
  they are ours to size; a vendor default is not an exemption. Scoped through
  `.map-slot` to outrank the library's own stylesheet, which arrives in the
  lazy map chunk and therefore loads later.

**And one that only a screenshot caught.** Forcing MapLibre's attribution
toggle to 44px made the measurement pass and broke the widget into a row of
overlapping duplicate icons spilling off the map — the library lays that
control out against its own dimensions. It is left at 24px, which clears AA.
A control that is the right size and visually broken is worse than one that
is small and works.

## Conventions for new work

1. **Never put a timer in a live region.** See above.
2. **Announce outcomes, not progress.** One `sr-only` polite region per
   meaningful change, not one per moving part.
3. **Name every region with a heading**, `sr-only` when the design does not
   want a visible one.
4. **24×24 minimum for anything clickable**, and give the hit area to the row
   rather than inflating the glyph.
5. **Never remove an outline** without replacing it with something of equal
   visibility. A `box-shadow` ring disappears in forced-colours mode.
6. **Pick the token for the job, not the colour** — see above.
7. **Colour is never the only signal.** The confidence indicator pairs its
   colour with a word and a count of filled segments; the freshness dot pairs
   its colour with text.

## Not checked

Stated plainly, because an accessibility document that lists only passes is
itself a kind of misleading.

- **No screen reader was run.** The live-region defect was found by counting
  DOM mutations, which is a proxy for what VoiceOver or NVDA would say, not a
  substitute. A real pass with VoiceOver on Safari and NVDA on Firefox is
  outstanding, and is the single most valuable remaining item here.
- **No axe or Lighthouse run**, and no automated checks in CI. Everything
  above was a one-off script against a live page, which means it does not
  protect against regression. The live-region rule in particular is the kind
  that comes back.
- **Keyboard traversal was not walked end to end.** Focus styling and scroll
  behaviour were verified; the full tab order through the comparison, the
  place-suggestion combobox and the breakdown disclosure was not.
- **The place-suggestion combobox was not audited against the ARIA authoring
  practices.** It reports `role="combobox"` and has a labelled listbox, and
  the interaction pattern — arrow keys, escape, selection announcement — has
  not been checked against the spec.
- **Only the comparison page was audited.** `/sources`, `/book` and the error
  states were not.
- **Zoom and reflow were not tested.** SC 1.4.10 at 400%, and SC 1.4.4 text
  resize, are unverified.
- **`npm run fills` measures fills against fills, not against everything.**
  It does not see an SVG `fill`/`stroke` attribute, a `::before` drawn as a
  background, a chart element under 6px in either direction, or a fill whose
  only neighbour is separated by a rule it cannot judge the visibility of. A
  border 1px wide in a colour close to both sides would pass it.
- **No testing with real assistive-technology users.**
